\subsection{From the original notebook to v2}
\label{sec:ablation-ladder}

We rebuilt the path from the original notebook to the final pipeline as a ladder of single changes and scored every rung on the same corrected test sets.
Task~2 rungs are trained with three seeds (42, 43, 44) and reported as mean $\pm$ sample standard deviation; Task~1 and Task~3 involve no training and are single deterministic runs.
Across the Task~2 rungs the seed standard deviation of test macro-F1 lies between 0.001 and 0.012, and the standard deviation of paired same-seed differences between 0.002 and 0.019, so we treat differences below about 0.01 as not established.
This noise floor is seed variability; the test-sample uncertainty of a paired Task~2 macro-F1 difference is wider (the 95\% bootstrap interval for image-only against L4a at seed 42 has a half-width of 0.035), which strengthens the not-established readings.

\paragraph{Task 2.}
Table~\ref{tab:t2_ladder} lists the rungs; all are evaluated on the full corrected test set of 2237 images.
The path fix is the only established accuracy gain.
The original notebook's \texttt{lstrip('data\_image/')} strips a set of characters instead of a prefix and silently dropped both earthquake events from the training and development data (L0).
Restoring them raises test macro-F1 from 0.494 $\pm$ 0.006 to 0.588 $\pm$ 0.012 (L1), a paired gain of $+0.095 \pm 0.011$ that is positive at all three seeds.
At seed 42 the paired bootstrap 95\% interval of this gain is $+0.051$ to $+0.149$, which excludes zero (computed from predictions regenerated under the original random-crop evaluation, which differ from the stored scores by less than 0.002).
Every later step moves test macro-F1 by less than the noise floor: the paired differences are $+0.004 \pm 0.002$ (QuickGELU CLIP, L2), $+0.008 \pm 0.007$ (10-epoch cap, L3), $-0.013 \pm 0.015$ (deterministic cached features, L3b), $+0.012 \pm 0.019$ (mixed precision, L4a) and $-0.011 \pm 0.010$ (randomly initialised text encoder, L4).
The rest of v2 therefore delivers equal accuracy at a fraction of the compute: L1 reaches 0.588 $\pm$ 0.012 and v2 (L4) 0.587 $\pm$ 0.001 test macro-F1, at 9.8 instead of 66.4 minutes of wall-clock time per run, about one seventh, measured on a single laptop GPU.
The whole bundle from L3 to L4 is consistently slightly lower ($-0.013 \pm 0.005$, negative at every seed), but no single step of it is established, including the effect of the random initialisation, which is no larger than the opposite-signed effect of mixed precision alone.
Development macro-F1 for L0 to L2 is inflated by selecting the best of 30 epochs on randomly cropped development images: it exceeds test macro-F1 by 0.063, 0.055 and 0.055 at L0, L1 and L2, against $-0.006$ at the 10-epoch rung L3.
Two implementation facts explain details of the ladder.
The original notebook applied a random crop to development and test images as well as to training images, which is why its CLIP features could not be cached before L3b.
v2's text encoder starts from random weights instead of pretrained DistilBERT: the cosine similarity between its saved word embeddings and the pretrained ones is $-0.0002$.

\paragraph{Task 3.}
Table~\ref{tab:t3_ladder} follows the nearest-neighbour index from the original notebook to v2; every rung is scored on the same 529 test queries and the three real damage classes.
The original index held all 18082 annotated images, including the test images, and 14556 of them (80.5\%) carried a ``Don't know'' label that never occurs in the test split.
The original notebook's score is not its real performance: with the test images in the index, all 529 test queries retrieve themselves among their five nearest neighbours, and the resulting macro-F1 of 0.688 (accuracy 0.722) falls to 0.378 (accuracy 0.503) once they are removed (L1).
L1 is therefore the fair measure of the original system; removing the polluted labels recovers 0.504 (L2), and the last two steps, dropping development images (L3) and same-tweet siblings (L4, v2), give 0.509 and 0.491, which are within the noise of 529 queries.

\subsection{Component ablation}
\label{sec:ablation-components}

\paragraph{Task 2.}
Table~\ref{tab:t2_partb} removes one component at a time from the L4a configuration (three seeds each; trainable parameters exclude the frozen CLIP encoder).
A 200k-parameter MLP on the cached CLIP vector scores 0.601 $\pm$ 0.005 test macro-F1 against 0.598 $\pm$ 0.010 for the full 67M-parameter model, a paired difference of $+0.003 \pm 0.009$, and concatenating the two streams without attention gives $-0.003 \pm 0.012$.
Neither difference is established.
The text stream alone reaches only 0.294 $\pm$ 0.014 ($-0.305 \pm 0.012$ against the full model), which is established.
Table~\ref{tab:a1} separates the test rows by whether the text and image annotations agree.
Over the whole test set the text adds no measurable macro-F1 gain (image-only minus the full model: $+0.003$).
The effect depends on whether the two labels agree: on the 955 rows where text and image labels agree, the full model exceeds image-only by $+0.021$ accuracy, positive at all three seeds and with a paired bootstrap 95\% interval of $+0.012$ to $+0.038$ at seed 42, so we regard the gain as established, while on the 1,282 disagreed rows it gives $-0.014$ macro-F1.
The text helps when it describes the same thing as the image, and the two effects largely cancel overall.
Removing the text-to-image attention block gives $-0.029 \pm 0.024$ (negative at all three seeds), which we regard as suggestive only.
That block attends over a single image vector, so its attention weight is identically one and it contributes an additional linear copy of the image; the image-to-text block and the direct residual image path remain in the ablated model.

\paragraph{Task 3.}
Tables~\ref{tab:t3_k}--\ref{tab:t3_emb} vary one factor of the nearest-neighbour classifier at a time on the tweet-disjoint index (single deterministic runs on 529 test queries).
The value $k=5$ is the best on the development set for the headline configuration (macro-F1 0.483), and test macro-F1 lies between 0.454 and 0.515 across $k \in \{1, 3, 5, 10, 20\}$, which is not an established effect.
Similarity-weighted voting gives metrics identical to majority voting in all 15 combinations of embedding and $k$, because the neighbours of a query have similar similarities (mean gap between the first and fifth neighbour 0.046).
Class-balanced voting raises development macro-F1 by 0.037 but test macro-F1 by only 0.004, and lowers accuracy from 0.667 to 0.586.
Image-only retrieval (0.507) is not worse than the fused embedding (0.491), and text-only retrieval is clearly weaker (0.428).
A logistic-regression probe on the same embeddings scores higher than five-nearest-neighbour voting on the fused embedding (0.615 against 0.491) and on the image embedding (0.595 against 0.507); the paired bootstrap 95\% intervals of the two macro-F1 differences, $+0.123$ ($+0.062$ to $+0.186$) and $+0.087$ ($+0.028$ to $+0.148$), both exclude zero.
The bootstrap resamples the 529 test queries for one index and one probe, so it does not cover variability from training.

\paragraph{Image encoder scale.}
We repeat the image-only Task~2 model, the Task~3 retrieval and probe, and the zero-shot classifier with CLIP ViT-L/14 in place of ViT-B/32 (Table~\ref{tab:b_scale}); the fusion model, the demo and all earlier results are unchanged.
ViT-L/14 differs from ViT-B/32 in both size (an image tower of 304M parameters against 88M) and patch size (14 against 32 pixels), so any gain is attributed to the larger CLIP model and not to size alone.
With the same head and recipe, the image-only Task~2 model gains 0.042 macro-F1 on average over three seeds (0.601 to 0.644), positive at all three seeds, and at seed 42 the paired bootstrap interval of the gain is $+0.001$ to $+0.063$, which only just excludes zero.
Feeding the raw L/14 features, the same input convention as B/32, gives 0.648 on average, and at seed 42 a gain of $+0.048$ ($+0.017$ to $+0.081$), so the L2 normalisation of the new embeddings does not explain the gain.
Against the full B/32 model (L4a) the L/14 image-only model is ahead by 0.029 at seed 42, but the interval ($-0.010$ to $+0.071$) includes zero.
For Task~3 the larger encoder helps the image-only paths (5-NN 0.507 to 0.548, logistic probe 0.595 to 0.621) but not the fused ones: the fused probe is 0.607 against 0.615 ($-0.008$, interval $-0.044$ to $+0.030$) and the fused 5-NN 0.505 against 0.491 ($+0.014$, interval $-0.035$ to $+0.063$), so neither fused difference is established, and the image-only differences were not bootstrapped.
Zero-shot macro-F1, with identical prompts and the prompt set chosen on the development split for both encoders, rises from 0.299 to 0.372 (accuracy 0.302 to 0.408); L/14 is above the stratified-random expectation of 0.333 and the always-``Severe damage'' baseline of 0.257, whereas B/32 was below the former, and it remains far below the trained probe (0.607).
Under the development-calibrated bias variant, which is not pure zero-shot, the ranking of the two encoders reverses (0.365 for L/14 against 0.397 for B/32), so only the statement that both are far below the trained probe is robust.
In short, the Task~2 gain is small but consistent across seeds, the fused Task~3 differences are not established, and none of these comparisons separates model scale from patch size.

\subsection{Robustness}
\label{sec:ablation-robustness}

\paragraph{Missing modality.}
Table~\ref{tab:c1} replaces one input at test time without retraining: a grey image, or an empty string.
A grey image collapses both Task~2 models to 0.080 (L4) and 0.081 (L4a) macro-F1, within 0.002 of the score of always predicting the majority class (0.0801) at every seed, so the models cannot fall back on the text alone.
A grey image is out of distribution for CLIP in the same way that an empty string is for DistilBERT.
Removing the text has a model-dependent effect: v2's model (L4) is unchanged ($+0.003$, 0.587 to 0.590), whereas the model with pretrained DistilBERT (L4a) loses between 0.058 and 0.103 macro-F1 across the three seeds (mean $-0.087$).
We read this as redundancy and not as an ignored input: text-only training reaches 0.294 and image-only training matches the full model, so the text does not supply information that the image lacks.
Training texts are never empty, so for an empty string the encoder sees only the start and end tokens, and the L4a drop may reflect that input shift as much as lost evidence.
For Task~3 a grey image costs 0.039 macro-F1 and an empty text 0.052 (single run).

\paragraph{Leave-one-disaster-out.}
Tables~\ref{tab:c2_t1} and~\ref{tab:c2_t3} train (Task~1) or build the index (Task~3) on six events and test on the seventh; the held-out event contributes no rows, which the code asserts for all 14 folds.
Averaged over the seven events, Task~1 macro-F1 falls from 0.714 (all events seen) to 0.680 and Task~3 from 0.381 to 0.354.
The pooled headline scores are 0.751 for Task~1 and 0.491 for Task~3; the per-event averages above differ from them because each event is scored separately, on its own class mix and size.
We observe that the Task~1 drop is concentrated in Sri Lanka floods, the only flood event (0.932 to 0.793), while five of the other six events change by less than 0.03.
For Task~3 the per-event changes have mixed signs (the largest drop is Hurricane Irma, 0.483 to 0.271), and three events have only 13 to 23 test images, so we regard the Task~3 figures as indicative only.
Task~2 is evaluated the same way (Table~\ref{tab:d_t2}) with the image-only model on cached CLIP features as a proxy for L4a, because the two tie over the whole test set (image-only minus L4a $+0.003 \pm 0.009$ macro-F1 over three seeds, with a paired bootstrap interval at seed 42 of $-0.036$ to $+0.034$); the full model was not run leave-one-event-out.
Each fold is run with seeds 42, 43 and 44, the checkpoint is selected on the development rows of the other six events only, and the code asserts that the held-out event contributes no train and no development rows.
Averaged over the seven events the macro-F1 falls from 0.614 to 0.572, and scoring all 2237 test rows at once gives 0.534 against 0.607 at seed 42, a difference of $-0.073$ with a paired bootstrap 95\% interval of $-0.108$ to $-0.036$, which excludes zero.
The loss is concentrated in the two earthquake events (Iraq--Iran 0.558 to 0.421, Mexico 0.660 to 0.568); the three hurricanes and the flood change by less than 0.02, and the wildfire change ($-0.026$) is within its seed spread ($\pm 0.099$) and is not interpretable.
The pooled drop is larger than the per-event average ($-0.072$ against $-0.042$ over three seeds) because macro-F1 weights the eight classes equally and one class carries most of it: Injured or dead people (10 test rows, 8 of them from the two earthquake events) falls from 0.609 to 0.190 F1 at seed 42, which is 72\% of the summed per-class loss, and without it the pooled drop at seed 42 would be $-0.020$.
The pooled bootstrap interval is therefore driven mainly by that one 10-row class (0.052 of the 0.073), and a bootstrap over a 10-row class gives only a rough interval.
L0 was trained without both earthquake events, so the L0 to L1 gain ($+0.095$) and the earthquake-fold losses above are two measurements of the same dependence on earthquake data.
In both experiments the same class dominates at seed 42: Injured or dead people, 8 of whose 10 test rows come from the two earthquake events, accounts for 56\% of the summed per-class gain from L0 to L1 and for 72\% of the summed per-class loss in the leave-one-disaster-out comparison.
No fold falls below its event's majority-class macro-F1 (0.064 to 0.174).

\paragraph{Confidence and calibration.}
We measure top-label expected calibration error (ECE, 15 equal-width bins) on the test split and fit one temperature per model on the development split, without any ECE threshold.
Task~2 is over-confident: the mean confidence of L4a is 0.867 against an accuracy of 0.799, and temperature scaling (T between 1.19 and 1.47) lowers its ECE from 0.070 to 0.023 on average over the three seeds.
The Task~3 probe is under-confident: its mean confidence is 0.607 against an accuracy of 0.684, and temperature scaling (T = 0.69) lowers its ECE from 0.077 to 0.038.
Both signals available to the hybrid track correctness: the probe is right on 0.812 of the 345 test queries where the five nearest neighbours agree with it and on 0.446 of the 184 where they disagree, and its accuracy rises monotonically over probe-confidence quintiles from 0.500 to 0.914.
As predictors of a correct answer, the probe confidence has an AUROC of 0.718 (95\% bootstrap interval 0.672 to 0.762) and agreement with the neighbours 0.692 (0.650 to 0.735).
The two signals are partly independent, so combining them, for example in a confidence score fitted on development data, is a natural direction for future work.
\paragraph{Duplicate images.}
Hashing every image file finds 181 groups of byte-identical files, 175 of them spanning different tweet ids.
Of the 529 Task~3 test images, 9 have an identical file in the Task~3 index and 24 have an index image at CLIP B/32 cosine of at least 0.98; of the 2,237 Task~2 test images, 68 have an identical file in the training or development split and 136 have a training image at that cosine.
Scoring the stored predictions without the flagged test rows (24 and 142) moves no macro-F1 by more than 0.005 (Task~3 hybrid 0.615 to 0.619, 5-NN 0.491 to 0.496; Task~2 mean over three seeds, L4a 0.599 to 0.597, image-only 0.601 to 0.599), and none of the five live demo images has an identical file in the index.
\paragraph{Class imbalance and extended metrics.}
Task~2 is severely imbalanced: the largest training class (``Not humanitarian'') has 6,565 rows and the smallest (``Missing or found people'') has 9, a ratio of 729.4:1 (Task~1: 2.4:1, Task~3: 4.6:1).
The treatments used before this study (balanced logistic regression for the Task~1 text model and the Task~3 probe, inverse-frequency class weights for Task~2) had never been compared with not using them.
Table~\ref{tab:g_arms} compares ten treatments with the image-only head on frozen CLIP features, for both image encoders, each scored once on the test split.
We select on the development split only: in a paired bootstrap on dev (2,000 resamples) seven of the ten arms are tied with the best arm on both encoders, and the rule fixed in advance (highest dev balanced accuracy among the tied arms) picks inverse-frequency weighting on B/32 and focal loss with inverse-frequency weights on L/14.
The encoders disagree, so the rule adopts no new loss: no treatment is established as better on macro-F1, and inverse-frequency weighting is retained for its recall and balanced-accuracy profile.
The rule has little power, since dev holds 8 injured-or-dead and 2 missing-or-found rows, so this does not show that inverse-frequency weighting is the best choice.

\paragraph{What is established.}
With three seeds and a noise floor of about 0.01 macro-F1, we regard only the following as established: the path-fix gain ($+0.095$), the collapse of the text-only model, the collapse of every Task~2 model without its image, the accuracy gain of the full model over image-only on the rows where the text and image labels agree, and the fall of the original Task~3 score once self-retrieval is removed.
Differences of about 0.01 between configurations are not established; this includes the effect of the random initialisation in v2 and of mixed precision, the comparison between image-only, concatenation and the full model, with a subset-dependent effect (A1), and all differences among the Task~3 rungs from L2 to L4.
The pooled Task~2 leave-one-disaster-out drop ($-0.073$ at seed 42, bootstrap interval excluding zero) is driven mainly by one 10-row class (0.052 of the 0.073); without it the drop is $-0.020$, and a bootstrap over a 10-row class gives only a rough interval.
\paragraph{Limitation.}
``Missing or found people'' has F1 = 0 in every Task~2 condition (3 test rows), which caps Task~2 macro-F1 at about 0.875 under the current models.
